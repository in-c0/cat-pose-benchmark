## 2. The performance claims, re-examined

All from `review/truth/methods/claims_audit.md`, generated from the committed aggregate by `python -m review.claims_audit`.

**Adjudication carries most of the holdout #2 number.** Holdout #2 was annotated before inference, which is the strongest design in the repository. After inference, the same model annotator checked overlays and added overrides. For v3 these set 24 frames to `correct` and 28 to `wrong_part`. Sixteen of the 24 are one clip (*katten naar buiten*), where the annotator concluded that its own blind reference boxes were 200–400 px off. That may be right. It is also exactly the judgement an independent reviewer should make, and nobody independent has. Scored as frozen, v3 on holdout #2 is precision 0.489 and recall 0.511. Adjudicated, it is 0.713 and 0.744. The README quoted only the second.

**Both holdouts pooled** (12 clips, the only data no decision was taken on), v3:

| scoring | TP / asserted / visible | precision | recall | median per-cat recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as frozen | 86 / 152 / 132 | 0.566 | 0.652 | 0.26 |
| adjudicated | 107 / 152 / 132 | 0.704 | 0.811 | 0.53 |

**Cross-cat generalisation is weak and poorly determined.** Six holdout cats have a visible tail. As frozen, v3's per-cat recall is 0.00, 0.08, 0.23, 0.29, 0.90 and 1.00. Two clips supply 76 of 86 true positives. *Larry* contributes 40 frames of one tail hanging still. *Katzengras* contributes 36, and note 05 itself says those masks are "the same kind of mask" as the whole-body false positives, on a cat whose tail happens to lie inside it. Resampling whole clips, the 95 % interval on pooled precision is 0.09–0.85 and on recall 0.16–0.92. Frame-level (Wilson) intervals are much narrower, because they treat 40 near-identical frames as 40 observations.

**False positives are long, consistent runs.** On holdout frames where the model truth says the tail is hidden, v3 asserted a tail on 36 of 240 (15 %). The errors are a few long runs, not scattered noise: the Taiwan rump (17 frames), the *Cito* face edge (11), and runs on *Andra and Billy* and *katten*. The temporal pass reinforces them, because it rewards consistency.

**Tail detection on the development clips** is the one place the method looks strong (0.89–0.92 precision, 0.85–0.88 recall). Those clips chose the method and shaped the truth, so the figure is an upper bound and not an estimate.

## 3. Reproduction of v1 → v3

All 16 clips were downloaded again from Wikimedia Commons, sampled with `review.sample_frames`, and run through `run_body`, `run_tail_grounded_v1`, `_v2` and `_v3` on an RTX 2080 (CUDA), with the code at `68044df` and no parameter changed. Wall time was 39 minutes, most of it one 4K download.

- **Frames:** the 147 dev frames are byte-identical to the committed digests. All 12 holdout clips give the same frame count and timestamps as the frozen truth files (456 frames).
- **Judgements:** scoring the re-run with `review.score_truth` reproduces every v3 frame judgement in the committed CSVs (603/603) and every v1 judgement except 4 (see below). No frame changed between asserted and refused.
- **Aggregate:** regenerating `aggregate.json` from the re-run gives identical v1 and v3 blocks for every set, both scorings and every clip. v3a was not re-run, so its blocks were not regenerated, and the committed aggregate is left as it was.
- **Record defects found on the way:** (1) `tail_grounded_v1+holdout2.csv` was stale. It said `wrong_part` on *Katzengras* f0–f3, where the override added in the same commit, and the aggregate, say `correct`. (2) `score_truth.py` kept the automatic `wrong_part=other` on rows an override set to `correct`, so 38 committed rows contradicted themselves. Both are fixed and the v1/v3 CSVs regenerated. No count changes.

So the pipeline reproduces. That shows the reported counts are what this code does on these frames. It says nothing about whether the labels the counts are scored against are right.

## 4. Discrepancies found, and what was done

| # | discrepancy | fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | README and issue #79 say 4 sheets, 57 frames, 285 rows; the template has 513 rows over 12 sheets and six methods. | README review section rewritten to point at HR1. The older sets are described with correct row counts. The #79 body update is drafted and waits for owner approval, because editing a public issue that a volunteer follows is outward-facing. |
| 2 | The human templates judge body, tail, anchored and grounded v0–v2. v3, the method the notes report, has no human review path. The prospective holdouts have none either. | HR1 covers v1 and v3 on all 12 holdout clips. |
| 3 | Two contradictory rules for uncertainty. The README said to leave unsure rows blank (ignored); review/README said "if in doubt, use `not_visible`" (counted). | review/README now separates the two: `not_visible` means the part cannot be seen, and blank means unsure about the model. HR1 uses an explicit `cannot_tell` and rejects blanks. |
| 4 | The old verdict review is not blind: the reviewer sees the model output and judges it, so visibility and correctness are judged together. | HR1 Stage A labels visibility on raw frames before any output is shown. Stage B shows outputs without method identity, shuffled, in one colour. |
| 5 | The submission channel breaks independence: CSVs posted as issue comments or PRs are visible to the next reviewer. | Hash-first locking; files are uploaded only after every reviewer has locked. |
| 6 | `review/score.py` reports agreement only for exactly two reviewers, as raw percent with no chance correction. With three reviewers it reported nothing. | Pairwise Cohen's kappa and Krippendorff's alpha added for two or more reviewers. HR1's scorer has its own tested implementation. |
| 7 | The human-verdict metric ("% ok of visible") cannot be compared with the truth-based precision/recall in the notes. | The HR1 scorer computes precision/recall with the definitions in `score_truth.py` and sets them next to the model-truth numbers. |
| 8 | review/README still called the walking and licking clips "held-out"; since note 05 they are development clips. | Corrected. |
| 9 | README headline quoted adjudicated holdout precision only. | As-frozen figure and intervals added, with a link here. |
| 10 | Holdout media and frames were never pinned by digest. | Frame digests are committed in `package/frames/`, with a note that they were pinned after the fact. |

## 5. What remains uncertain after this audit

- Whether the model truth agrees with people. HR1 R2 answers this.
- Whether the *katten* and *Katzengras* overrides are right. HR1 reports each override as upheld or overturned.
- Whether v3's 40 *Katzengras* "correct" frames put the curve on the tail or along the body. Stage B asks this directly.
- Anything at curve level (shape, length, pixel error). HR1 does not measure it; note 06 §5 describes the experiment that would.
- Performance on owner-shot home video. Every clip here is from Wikimedia Commons.
