# Sixteen passes: what was proposed, what was tested, and what survived

**Ava Kim** — cat-pose-benchmark, technical note 06, v0.2, 20 September 2026 (v0.2: criteria in Table 1 corrected against the pass log after the reviewer's audit; passes 17–20 added)

Repository: https://github.com/in-c0/cat-pose-benchmark · Follows note 05 · Licence of this note: CC-BY-4.0

## Abstract

This is the audit trail behind note 05. Over one night, twenty passes with a reviewer model produced sixteen proposals for improving the grounded tail method were made by a reviewer model, built, tested against a written-down criterion, and kept or dropped on the result. One survived: the two-sided SAM2 bridge (v3). Everything else — denser sampling, re-weighted temporal terms, a wider candidate lattice, a lower detector threshold, a looser gate, a cheaper switch, tracking until failure, a consensus audit, two forms of zero-shot semantic verification and one of mask geometry — failed the test it was given, and the table below says which test and by how much. The frame truth was revised four times on the way, and those revisions are in the same table, because three of them were found by method output and the record should say so. The pass log with every proposal and reply in full is in the portfolio hub. §5 is the reviewer's own closing reading: measure the oracle ceiling of the current stack on human curve truth before choosing the next component to change.

## 1. Why a table

Note 05 reports the outcome. It does not show how many things were tried and dropped to get there, and a reader deciding whether to try one of them again needs the negative results as much as the positive one. Each row below is one pass: what the reviewer proposed, what was built, the criterion written down before the result was known, the number, and the disposition. Where the criterion was not written down first, the row says so.

## 3. What the table says

Three things. First, the only change that survived is the one that adds no threshold and no new model: it asks two propagations to agree and refuses when they do not. Second, the truth moved four times, three of them because a method found something the annotator had not, and each time the fix was to make the truth stricter or to exclude, never to accept the method's reading. Third, the failures cluster: every semantic and structural verifier tried was a per-frame test of a crop or a mask, and every one failed because true tails and false assertions overlap on whatever single axis was chosen — a hanging cream tail is what the zero-shot model calls a leg, a curled grey tail is as body-attached as a whole-cat mask. The reviewer model's own reading at the end of pass 16 was to stop tuning here, and that is what this note does. Its audit at pass 19 then found that the scorer had been judging the detector's box rather than the curve the method reports, which hid six collapsed centrelines; the corrected numbers are in note 05 v0.3 and do not change any disposition in Table 1. One limitation stands over every number: the 603 frames come from 16 clips and are not 603 independent observations — one clip supplies 40 near-identical easy tails, another a 17-frame repeated mistake — so the aggregate also reports per-clip counts, a macro average over clips and the number of distinct false runs (seven on holdout #2, not 27).

## 4. What comes next

The reviewer model's proposal for the next phase was a linear probe on the frozen SigLIP image embedding of the existing crop, trained on the 237 accepted predictions (191 true, 46 false) with each clip and each contiguous same-label run given equal weight, evaluated by exhaustive leave-two-clips-out, threshold chosen on training clips only as the highest that keeps 98 % of weighted training true positives, and promoted only if held-out false assertions fall by 25 % with true ones down no more than 2 %, on at least three different held-out clips, with no clip of ten or more true frames losing more than one. It was proposed for 16 clips and 120 folds; only 11 clips have an accepted frame, so the executed experiment was 55 folds. It was run as an exploratory probe, because the labels are model-annotated and a human has not checked them (that item is filed and open), and it failed every clause: held-out true frames fell from 191 to 44 while false ones fell from 46 to 15. With one cat per clip, a probe trained on ten cats' tails does not recognise the eleventh's, which is note 04's result again at a larger scale. The frozen embedding is not sufficient at this data size; the sequence stops there rather than escalating to fine-tuning.

## 5. What the next investigator should do first

This section is the reviewer model's closing reading of the whole sequence, asked for as the twentieth and last pass and reproduced here as written (ChatGPT, 20 September 2026), with two light edits for house style. It is the reviewer's view, attributed as such, and I agree with it.

> The next investigator should not begin with another prompt, threshold, verifier or temporal rule. The first job is measurement.
>
> The present benchmark has established something narrower but useful. Grounding DINO can often point at an extended tail. SAM2 can often turn a good prompt into a usable tail mask and can bridge short gaps when independent propagation from both sides agrees. But the system does not know reliably whether a plausible elongated region is a tail. Across the two prospective holdouts, the dominant errors were not random detector noise: they were stable, visually plausible alternatives — rump, whole cat, forepaw or leaf, face edge, and once a person's face. Temporal consistency reinforces those mistakes because they are consistent. Zero-shot SigLIP, tighter crops, simple mask geometry and a linear probe on frozen SigLIP embeddings all failed predeclared tests. The evidence so far does not support another rule layered on top.
>
> Before training anything, a human should verify the existing truth. More importantly, the next data collection should finally annotate the object this project claims to benchmark: the continuous tail curve itself. A fresh set of cats should have per-instance visible/partial/hidden labels and human tail centrelines or masks, with ambiguous cases independently reviewed. The existing box truth is useful for asking whether an output is somewhere on the tail, but it cannot tell whether the reported curve has the right shape.
>
> **The one measurement that would change the diagnosis.** The most informative next experiment is the oracle ceiling of the current pipeline. On fresh human curve-level truth, retain every tail proposal produced by the current Grounding DINO stage before the v1 selection machinery removes candidates. Run the existing SAM2 segmentation and centreline extraction for every proposal. Then, using the ground truth only for analysis, allow an oracle to choose the best resulting curve on each frame, or NONE when no tail should be reported. This asks: if candidate selection were perfect, how good could the current detector + SAM2 + centreline stack possibly be? Measure the distance between each oracle-selected curve and the human curve, preferably with a scale-normalised curve metric rather than another box threshold, and retain the reason when the oracle cannot recover the tail: no usable detector proposal, segmentation failure, or centreline failure. Compare that oracle result with v3 on the same frames. That gap determines what should happen next.
>
> If the oracle is very good and v3 is much worse, the components already contain the answer and selection is the bottleneck; collect substantially more cats and train a tail-specific selector or verifier with clip- and animal-disjoint evaluation. The failed eleven-clip linear probe says that the present data are not enough; it does not show that supervised selection is impossible. If the oracle itself misses many visible tails because no proposal reaches the real tail, stop working on verification: the detector is the ceiling; compare or train a tail-specific detector on curve-annotated data. If a good proposal exists but SAM2 frequently expands to body, leg or background, segmentation is the ceiling; train or adapt a tail segmenter instead of trying to classify its failures afterwards. If the mask is correct but the extracted centreline is wrong or collapses, the representation is the ceiling; evaluate the tail directly as a mask or ordered spline and replace the current skeletonisation step. This oracle experiment should be performed before a new detector bake-off or any fine-tuning, because otherwise several components will change at once and a gain will not identify what was limiting the system.
>
> **What not to repeat.** The negative results in this note should be treated as evidence, not unfinished tuning. Do not repeat global detector-threshold lowering without a new reason: hidden-tail frames already produce abundant false tail proposals. Do not lower the Viterbi switch cost to rescue isolated tails: the first recovered frame arrived with the first new false positive. Do not assume a higher frame rate improves the temporal model: fixed-timestamp evaluation reversed that conclusion. Do not extend one-sided SAM2 tracking merely because its mask remains non-empty: it can track a hind leg confidently for several frames. Do not add another zero-shot negative prompt because one failure happens to resemble that category: true tails already score as leg, belly or nearly zero depending on the animal and scene. Do not expect a single area, attachment or elongation threshold to separate tails from false masks: the observed true and false modes occupy both ends of those axes. And do not train another verifier on the present model-annotated crops and call cross-frame volume a sample size; the eleven active clips behaved like eleven domains, not 237 independent examples.
>
> The other lesson is methodological. Preserve prospective data. Freeze truth before inference. Keep post-inference adjudication separate from as-frozen scoring. Group errors by clip and contiguous run as well as by frame. When a method exposes a truth error, make the truth stricter or uncertain rather than silently moving it toward the prediction. And keep negative experiments; several of the most useful results in these twenty passes were proposals that failed.
>
> **Where the ceiling is today.** I would not yet call the current performance a model ceiling. It is a zero-shot-composition ceiling on the evidence collected so far. The surviving v3 bridge demonstrates that temporal information can recover short missing observations without another learned model or fitted threshold. The two prospective holdouts demonstrate that its larger limitation is semantic identity: deciding that a particular visible structure is a tail at all. The failed zero-shot and frozen-embedding verifiers show that this decision is not captured robustly by the representations and small sample available here. The next phase should therefore be data-first, not rule-first. Human curve truth plus the current-stack oracle ceiling would tell us whether the next dollar and hour belong to candidate selection, detection, segmentation or curve extraction. Until that measurement exists, choosing among those directions would be another guess.

That is where the twenty-pass sequence stops.
