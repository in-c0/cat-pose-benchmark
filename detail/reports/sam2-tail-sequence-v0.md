# Tail sequence propagation v0 — geometry succeeds; identity re-acquisition remains

Status: **single-frame tail geometry remains feasible; pure sequence propagation requires revision**

This experiment asks a narrower question than the earlier single-frame test:

> Once a tail-only mask is available on one good frame, can a generic video segmenter preserve that tail identity across motion, occlusion and a second cat without re-annotation?

The answer on the first real sequence is: **partly, but not robustly enough**.

## Source and seed

- source: Wikimedia Commons `Cat jumping backwards.webm`, CC-BY-3.0;
- original-motion window: first 8 seconds;
- evaluation sampling: 1.5 fps, 12 frames;
- seed frame: index 5, approximately 3.333 s;
- seed endpoints: machine-generated SuperAnimal-Quadruped R3 evidence from run `31891814198`;
- frozen seed fixture: `detail/fixtures/superanimal-jumping-tail-seed-frame5.json`.

The frozen prompt is retained for reproducibility only. It is not a human label, independent reference or product-cleared training target.

## Important negative control: point-seeded video propagation

Initial sequence run: `31917300993`.

The first implementation passed SuperAnimal tail/base-tip points directly to `SAM2VideoPredictor.add_new_points_or_box`.

The workflow completed, but visual QA showed a semantic failure: **the video predictor expanded to a whole-cat/body mask even on the conditioning frame**. A naive coverage metric therefore gave a misleading pass.

This negative control changed the experiment design:

1. use the SAM2 image predictor on the seed frame;
2. evaluate its multiple image-mask candidates against positive tail and negative body prompts;
3. select the tail-only mask;
4. pass that explicit binary mask into `SAM2VideoPredictor.add_new_mask`;
5. require the video model to preserve the seed mask before evaluating temporal propagation.

The feasibility gate was also tightened to reject:

- poor seed-mask preservation;
- body-scale mask expansion;
- empty masks;
- degenerate near-zero-length pseudo-curves.

## Visual QA

The sequence contact sheet shows a clear regime change:

- frames roughly 1–8: the mask and deterministic centreline generally follow the grey cat's visible tail;
- frame 9: propagation drops out;
- frame 10: the tracked tail identity is lost as a second cat becomes salient; the output attaches to the wrong animal/region and collapses to a degenerate curve;
- frame 11: the segmentation has migrated to the foreground cat's head/upper body rather than the original grey cat's tail.

Therefore the residual failure is **not evidence that a continuous-tail representation requires a dedicated neural tail head**. The centreline extractor works when the tail mask is semantically correct. The failure is target identity / occlusion re-acquisition in a multi-cat scene.

## Teacher re-seeding check

A simple periodic re-seed from the same SuperAnimal teacher was considered and rejected as the immediate fix.

On the failure window, the single-individual teacher itself changes semantic target. At frame 10 its nominal high-confidence tail endpoints land on the foreground cat/chair region, not on the original grey cat's tail. Blind re-seeding would therefore reinforce the identity switch.

This establishes an important boundary:

> Per-frame pose confidence is insufficient for feline identity continuity in multi-cat video.

## Decision

### Tail geometry — keep the compositional approach

**NO-GO remains on a dedicated learned tail network for now.**

The evidence supports:

```text
valid tail endpoints / tail-only seed
→ generic segmentation
→ deterministic skeleton / geodesic extraction
→ continuous tail centreline
```

The remaining tail work should measure real curve accuracy with sparse independent visible-tail labels and harder single-cat occlusion cases.

### Temporal identity — move to #12

The new blocking residual belongs to the temporal/world-space layer:

```text
multi-cat scene
→ persistent target identity
→ occlusion detection
→ re-acquisition without switching animal
→ tail/body detail continuation
```

The next temporal baseline should be identity-aware and should not trust the highest-confidence per-frame animal skeleton as identity evidence by itself.

Candidate minimal approaches before a large learned temporal model:

1. maintain a separate whole-animal identity track and constrain tail masks to that track;
2. multi-individual pose inference + assignment against prior body geometry/appearance;
3. confidence-aware re-acquisition only after explicit identity matching;
4. periodic tail-only image re-seeding conditioned on the persistent identity track.

## Scientific boundary

All SuperAnimal prompts, SAM2 masks and derived curves in this experiment are **R3 model-derived evidence**.

- Visual QA establishes semantic plausibility/failure, not metric curve accuracy.
- The sequence does not establish independent ground truth.
- The outputs are not automatically cleared as product-training labels.
- Visible tail centreline remains a surface-geometry representation, not internal vertebral anatomy.
